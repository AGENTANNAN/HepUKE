Study the neutron electromagnetic form factors via e+e− → n nbar in the continuum, using the 12 scan points from 2.0000 to 2.9500 GeV (2.0000, 2.0500, 2.1250, 2.1500, 2.1750, 2.2000, 2.2324, 2.3094, 2.3864, 2.3960, 2.6454, 2.9500) grouped into 5 c.m. energy intervals for the extraction. Use the corresponding real data and inclusive MC at each point, and generate 500k-event signal exclusive MC per energy point with the ConExc NLO generator (ISR plus vacuum polarization, Coulomb factor 1) for e+e− → n nbar. This is a fully neutral final state: require zero charged tracks in the MDC, an EMC shower with deposited energy between 0.5 and 2.0 GeV and |cosθ|<0.7 as the nbar candidate, and TOF hits within the azimuthal span of six scintillators along the neutron momentum, with events classified into three categories — A (TOF and EMC shower from both n and nbar), B (EMC from both, TOF from nbar only), C (EMC from both, no TOF). Signal yields per cosθ_nbar bin (7 equidistant bins within −0.7 < cosθ_nbar < 0.7) come from a composite maximum-likelihood fit (NLL minimization with MIGRAD), with beam-related and e+e− → γγ backgrounds. Neutron/antinbar efficiencies are calibrated data-driven using J/ψ → p̄πn (pπn̄) control samples (10087M J/ψ events), plus a trigger correction from the EMC-based neutral trigger and an iterative form-factor-model efficiency determination converging within 1%. Finally, |G_M| and R_em = |G_E|/|G_M| are extracted by a simultaneous Poisson-PDF fit to the cosθ differential cross sections of the three categories.
