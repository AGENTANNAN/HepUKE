At √s = 3.686 GeV, study ψ(3686) → Ω−Ω̄+ with a single-tag method, reconstructing one Ω and inferring the recoiling one from the missing mass. Use the 3.686 GeV real data set (709_3686) and inclusive MC, and generate one exclusive signal MC sample of 1,000,000 events for ψ(3686) → Ω−Ω̄+, with Ω− → K−Λ, Λ → pπ− and the charge-conjugate chain, initially phase-space and then weighted by measured helicity amplitudes for efficiency. For the Ω− tag, require charged tracks with |cosθ|<0.93, |Vz|<10 cm, Vr<1 cm, at least one positive and at least two negative tracks; identify one proton against K/π and then one K− against π/p by the probability method with a 0.001 threshold, taking the remaining negative track as π−. Apply pT > 0.2 GeV for the proton, > 0.1 GeV for the K−, and > 0.05 GeV for the π−, perform a Λ → pπ− secondary vertex fit, combine K−Λ requiring the mass closest to the nominal Ω mass of 1.67245 GeV/c², and require the recoil Ω̄ mass in [1.640, 1.692] GeV/c². The Ω̄+ tag is the charge-conjugate selection: at least two positive and one negative track, one anti-proton then one K+ by the same PID, the remaining positive track as π+, the same pT cuts, an anti-Λ vertex fit, and the same recoil-mass window. The Λ and Ω mass windows are [1.110, 1.122] GeV/c² and [1.663, 1.681] GeV/c², respectively, implemented via closest-to-nominal-mass combination; no global nC kinematic fit or χ² cut is applied, and both single-tag selections are run on data and the inclusive and exclusive MC samples.
