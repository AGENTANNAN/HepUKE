Using the seven energy points from 4.600 to 4.699 GeV, measure the branching fractions of Λc+ → pη and Λc+ → pω with the single-tag, direct-reconstruction method. The inputs are the real data at all seven points plus the inclusive MC at 4.680 GeV, together with 100k-event exclusive signal MC for each of three modes: Λc+ → pη with η → γγ, Λc+ → pη with η → π+π−π0 (π0 → γγ), and Λc+ → pω with ω → π+π−π0 (π0 → γγ). Charged tracks must satisfy |cosθ| < 0.93, |Vz| < 10 cm and Vr < 1 cm; photons are required to have 25 MeV (barrel) / 50 MeV (endcap) EMC energy, EMC TDC within 0–14, be at least 8° away from any charged track, and be at least two per event. Particle ID uses the probability method (prob > 0.001), separating p from π/K and π from p/K: the η → γγ mode requires exactly 1 proton (no negative track, net charge 1) with an extra proton Vr < 0.2 cm, while the η → π+π−π0 and ω → π+π−π0 modes require exactly 1 p, 1 π+ and 1 π− (2 positive, 1 negative, net charge 1), again with proton Vr < 0.2 cm. In the three-pion modes, the two photons are first fitted with a π0 mass constraint (χ² < 50) and required to have M(γγ) in 0.115–0.150 GeV, then π+π−π0 are combined into an η or ω with mass in 0.536–0.560 GeV or 0.750–0.810 GeV (with Dalitz R < 0.9 for ω), whereas the η → γγ mode instead uses a 1C fit of the two photons to the η mass (χ² < 20), keeping one η. Each of the three modes is finally subjected to a nominal four-momentum-constrained fit to pη or pω with χ² < 200, and the yields are extracted by unbinned maximum-likelihood fits to M_BC versus ΔE for the η modes and to M_BC for the ω mode (ΔE within [−0.03, 0.02] GeV).
