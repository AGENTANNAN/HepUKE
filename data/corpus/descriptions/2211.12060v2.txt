Search for the hidden-charm tetraquark Z_cs'− in e+e− → K+ D_s*− D*0 at 4.661, 4.682 and 4.699 GeV, with D_s*− → D_s− γ, D_s− → K+K−π−, D*0 → D0π0, D0 → K−π+, and π0 → γγ. Use the corresponding real data and inclusive MC, and generate 500k-event exclusive MC for the non-resonant signal at each scan point with default cross section. Two partial-reconstruction tags are used: the D_s− tag reconstructs the bachelor K+ plus D_s− → K+K−π− (or K_S0 K− with K_S0 → π+π−, M = 0.485–0.511 GeV, L/σ_L > 2), missing the D*0; the D*0 tag reconstructs the bachelor K+ plus D*0 → D0π0, D0 → K−π+, missing the D_s*−. Both share charged tracks with |cosθ|<0.93, |Vz|<10 cm, Vr<1 cm, at least two positive and two negative tracks, and photons with EMC TDC 0–10, barrel/endcap energy thresholds 25/50 MeV and angle to tracks >10° (at least two photons in the D*0 tag); PID uses the probability method with prob > 0.001, first identifying pions against kaons and removing them, then identifying kaons against pions requiring at least one K+ and one K−. For the D_s− tag, require a D_s− mass window of 1.955–1.980 GeV (best candidate at 1.9683 GeV), M(K+K−) < 1.05 GeV or 0.850 < M(K+π−) < 0.930 GeV, and recoil mass RM(K+D_s−) in 2.11–5.0 GeV/c² after substituting the PDG D_s− mass. For the D*0 tag, require a kinematic fit of two photons to the π0 nominal mass with χ² < 25 and at least one π0, a D0 mass window 1.850–1.880 GeV (best at 1.8648 GeV), a D*0 mass window 2.000–2.014 GeV (best at 2.00685 GeV), and RM(K+D*0) in 2.102–2.122 GeV/c² after PDG D*0-mass correction. Both algorithms use a fixed ECMS constant of 4.682 GeV and run on all scan data, inclusive MC and exclusive MC.
