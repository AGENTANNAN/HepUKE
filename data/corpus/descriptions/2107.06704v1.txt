On the J/ψ (√s = 3.097 GeV), measure the branching fraction of Λ → p μ⁻ ν̄_μ (and its charge conjugate) with the double-tag technique: one Λ is reconstructed as the tag in Λ → p̄π⁺ (Λ̄ → pπ⁻) and the other as the signal Λ → p μ⁻ ν̄_μ. Use the 3.097 GeV real data and inclusive MC, together with 100k-event exclusive MC for J/ψ → ΛΛ̄ with the Λ decaying 50/50 to pπ⁻ and pμ⁻ν̄_μ (and the conjugate modes for Λ̄). Charged tracks are required to have |cosθ| < 0.93, |Vz| < 10 cm, Vr < 1 cm, with exactly two positive and two negative tracks; PID uses the probability method (prob > 0.001) identifying protons against kaons/pions and muons against electrons/pions/kaons/protons, requiring at least one p, one p̄ and one μ. Protons and antiprotons are then removed to leave the pions, and secondary vertex fits (minimizing the mass difference) build the Λ from p̄π⁺ and the Λ̄ from pπ⁻, followed by a 4C kinematic fit on the ΛΛ̄ pair. On the tag side choose the minimum |ΔE| in [-17, 13] MeV with M_BC in [1.089, 1.143] GeV/c², a tag vertex χ² < 100 and decay length > 2σ from the IP, while for the signal require N_track = 4, a muon with L_μ > 0.001 and L_μ > L_e, and veto Λ → pπ⁻ by requiring the 4C fit χ² > 20 (signal fails it because of the missing neutrino), M_recoil(Λ̄ p) > 0.170 GeV/c² and M_{pμ}(4C) in [1.075, 1.100] GeV/c². The DT yield is extracted from a fit to U_miss = E_miss − c|p_miss| (peaking at zero for signal), giving the branching fraction separately for Λ and Λ̄ and hence the CP asymmetry.
