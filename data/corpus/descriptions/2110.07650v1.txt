Study the amplitude analysis of Ds+ → KS0 KS0 π+ at √s = 4.178–4.226 GeV, using the double-tag technique in which the tag-side Ds− is reconstructed in eight hadronic modes (KS0K−, K+K−π−, K+K−π−π0, KS0K−π−π+, KS0K+π−π−, π−π−π+, π−η′ and K−π+π−, with the two charge-conjugate KS0Kππ states sharing one decay symbol and the π−η′ mode handled through a π−η proxy). Use the real data and inclusive MC samples at all six energy points 4.178, 4.189, 4.199, 4.209, 4.219 and 4.226 GeV. Reconstruct the tag locally with the beam energy taken from the database. Perform an 8C kinematic fit — four-momentum conservation plus the two KS0 masses, the tag Ds mass, the signal Ds mass and the Ds* mass — with a χ² cut of 200, noting that the code itself imposes the 4C and Ds mass constraints while the KS0 and Ds* mass constraints are applied in the BOSS job options. Apply a signal Ds+ invariant-mass window of [1.950, 1.990] GeV/c². The branching-fraction measurement uses the double-tag selection without any kinematic fit, requiring the soft pion momentum to exceed 0.1 GeV/c to suppress D*+ decays; the amplitude analysis itself is an isobar-model fit performed in ROOT.
