Measure the τ mass from an energy scan near the τ+τ− threshold, studying e+e− → τ+τ− in thirteen two-prong final states (ee, eμ, eπ, eK, μμ, μπ, μK, ππ, πK, KK, plus eρ, μρ and πρ, with ρ→ππ0, π0→γγ and the τ-decay neutrinos undetected). Use the real data and inclusive MC at four scan points with CM energies 3542.4, 3553.8, 3561.1 and 3600.2 MeV (the first, below threshold, serving as a pure-background control); generate exclusive MC at every scan point, 200k events for the ten photon-less two-prong channels and 200k for the three Xρ channels, plus 500k τ-pair events per point. Event selection requires exactly one positive and one negative track with |cosθ|<0.93, |Vz|<10 cm and Vr<1 cm; the ten two-prong channels allow no photons, while the Xρ channels require exactly two photons with barrel (endcap) energy thresholds 25 (50) MeV, EMC timing 0–750 ns, more than 20° to the nearest track, and a one-constraint kinematic fit constraining the two photons to the π0 mass (χ²<25, at least one π0), with the γγ mass in 112.8–146.4 MeV and the ρ (ππ0) mass in 376.5–1195.5 MeV. PID uses the probability method with a 0.001 cut, treating high-momentum tracks (p>0.2 GeV/c) as leptons (electron if EMC energy >0.6 GeV, else muon) and separating π from K; the undetected τ-decay neutrinos are handled by a missing-mass constraint requiring the recoil mass to be 1.60–1.90 GeV for the two-prong channels and 1.55–1.90 GeV for the Xρ channels, while channel-dependent PTEM and acoplanarity windows are applied offline on the NTuple.
