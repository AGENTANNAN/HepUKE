At ψ(3770) (√s = 3.773 GeV), study e+e− → D D̄ with a double-tag method: one D is reconstructed as an ST tag, while the other is the signal D → η + hadrons with η → γγ, using the 3.773 GeV real data and its inclusive MC. Two tag analyses are run: for the D+-tag side six hadronic modes (K−π+π+, K_S0 π+, K−π+π+π0, K_S0 π+π0, K_S0 π+π+π−, K+K−π+) leave a D̄0 signal, and for the D0-tag side three modes (K−π+, K−π+π0, K−π+π+π−) leave a D+ signal; only the signal modes available in the DTag machinery are used (six D0 and five D+ modes, the rest dropped). Signal-side events require exactly two photons with an opening angle above 10° and at least one charged track. The η → γγ candidate must satisfy |M(γγ) − m(η)| < 27.5 MeV, a K_S0 → π+π− is required with L/σ_L > 2 and mass in (0.486, 0.510) GeV, and mass-window vetos are applied against K_S0 (0.468–0.528 and 0.438–0.538 GeV), η (0.498–0.578), ω (0.732–0.832), η′ (0.908–1.008) and φ (0.990–1.390), with per-mode ΔE_sig requirements and a peaking-background veto for the Kππ0η modes. A 1C Kalman kinematic fit is performed imposing four-momentum conservation with the γγ invariant mass constrained to the nominal η mass, χ² < 200, and the beam energy fixed to 1.8865 GeV. The best tag candidate is the one with the smallest |ΔE_tag|, and the opening angle between the signal and tag D is required to exceed 160°.
