On the ψ(3686), study ψ(3686) → p pbar π0, π0 → γγ, giving a p pbar γγ final state. Use the 3.686 GeV real data (106M events, 160 pb^-1), its inclusive MC, the 3.650 GeV continuum data (42 pb^-1) and its inclusive MC; generate 100k-event phase-space exclusive MC for ψ(3686) → p pbar π0 and 100k for the continuum e+e− → γ* → p pbar π0. Select charged tracks with |cosθ|<0.8, |Vz|<20 cm, Vr<2 cm, exactly one positive and one negative track, and net charge zero. Photons must satisfy EMC timing 0–14, be at least 10° from the proton, exceed 25 MeV (barrel) or 50 MeV (endcap), and number at least two; isolated photons must be at least 10° from the proton and 30° from the antiproton, again at least two. Identify particles by the probability method with a 0.001 probability cut, separating protons from kaons and pions, and require exactly one proton and one antiproton. Perform a 4C kinematic fit to the p pbar γγ system, then a nominal 5C fit adding the π0 mass constraint on the two photons, with χ²<200, retaining the smallest-χ² combination when more than two photons are present. Additional mass windows are the J/ψ veto |M(p pbar)-M(J/ψ)|>40 MeV/c² and the π0 sideband 30<|M(γγ)-135|<45 MeV/c², evaluated with fit-corrected four-momenta. Both signal and continuum exclusive MC use phase-space decays.
