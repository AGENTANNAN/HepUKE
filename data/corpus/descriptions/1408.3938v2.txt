On the J/ψ (3.097 GeV), study J/ψ → p p̄ a0(980), a0(980) → π0 η with π0 → γγ and η → γγ, using the J/ψ real data sample (2.25×10^8 events) and its inclusive MC. Generate exclusive MC of 100k events for the signal J/ψ → p p̄ a0(980) → p p̄ π0 η and 500k phase-space J/ψ → p p̄ π0 η events to derive the mass-dependent efficiency curve. Event selection requires charged tracks with |cosθ|<0.93, |Vr|<1 cm, |Vz|<10 cm, at least one positive and one negative track; photons with E>25 MeV in the barrel and E>50 MeV in the end caps, EMC timing between 0 and 14 (50 ns units), and at least four photons. PID uses the probability method with dE/dx only, identifying a (anti)proton when its probability exceeds both the kaon and pion probabilities, requiring at least one proton and one antiproton. A 4C kinematic fit with four-momentum conservation is applied to p p̄ γγ γγ, choosing the four-photon combination with the smallest χ², with a loose χ²<200 cut (a tighter χ²<35 is applied later). The four photons are then paired into π0 and η by minimizing a χ²-like mass variable, the resulting π0 and η masses must fall within 3σ windows, and a π0π0 veto rejects events with minimum π0π0 χ²<100.
